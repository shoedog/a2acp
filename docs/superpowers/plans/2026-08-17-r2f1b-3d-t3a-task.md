# R2f1b 3d — T3a: the exact-absence proof and its tri-state refusal (decide, do not act)

## Description

Third of the 3d tasks, first of two halves. Base: `main` `1d7826dd` (T1 and T2
both landed — the tree already contains `PreparationFlightStateV1::Settled`, the
claimed materialization flight, the single publication phase CAS, the
backend-owned control root, and `preparation_recovery_flights`, T2's recovery
inventory. Read them first and build on them; do not rework them).

Authority: slice-3 brief §3d(c) and (d), restated below. You need no other
document.

**Why this task is half of 3d's third task.** §3d(c)+(d) as written bundles the
absence proof, an async/trait recovery seam, a refusing lock window, a
descriptor-safe removal, and a two-population marker authority into one task.
On this lane a slice that size does not converge inside a review cap: T2 was 682
delivered lines and consumed four review rounds, a park, an extension and two
operator completions. Splitting before dispatch rather than after rejection is
the standing discipline, so:

- **T3a (this task) DECIDES.** It builds the exact-absence proof, the recovery
  seam it needs, and the tri-state refusal — and performs **no record mutation
  whatsoever**. Its output is a typed decision value.
- **T3b (next) ACTS.** It consumes that decision under the refusing lock window
  spanning proof→transition→unlink, publishes the `UnusedSettled` transition,
  performs the descriptor-safe removal, and serves the marker population.

Landing an effect-free decision first is safe by construction and matches lane
precedent (`custody_writer` is documented production-unreachable at
`custody_writer.rs:20-27`).

## What T3a delivers

**1. The exact-absence proof, state-agnostic.** A candidate may be settled as
unused only on proof that the target does *not* exist. Per §3d(d) the proof is
keyed on **state-agnostic exact absence** so that ONE definition serves both the
2b2 marker population and the candidate population. It must add **no edge** to
the frozen custody transition table (`crates/bridge-worktree/src/custody.rs`).
`UnusedSettled {}` already exists at `custody.rs:132`, and the table already
records that it precedes `git worktree add` entirely and therefore may carry a
degraded identity (`custody.rs:209-219`) — do not change those rulings.

**2. The B18 async/trait recovery seam, and a design note justifying it.** The
shapes are mismatched and that mismatch is the task's real design content:

- the sweep is **sync**: `sweep_orphans(root, my_host, probe: &dyn LeaseProbe)`
  at `crates/bridge-worktree/src/sweep.rs:311`;
- the registration probe is **async and private**: `registration_absent` at
  `crates/bridge-worktree/src/host_git.rs:114`, which shells out to
  `git worktree list --porcelain` and treats any failure as `Err`.

Choose the seam and write down why, in a handoff design note the review will
check. The existing `LeaseProbe` trait is the obvious precedent for a sync
trait seam over a host capability. Whatever you choose must not block a runtime
worker thread on an async call in a way that can deadlock, and must not make the
sweep's sync contract a lie.

**3. Tri-state refusal, fail-closed.** The proof answers exactly three ways:

| Answer | Meaning | Decision |
|--------|---------|----------|
| target present | the checkout exists | **refuse** |
| registered-but-absent | target gone, registration remains | **refuse** |
| both absent, proven | target and registration both provably gone | authorize settlement |
| cannot prove | probe failed, ambiguous, or unavailable | **refuse** |

`Err` from the probe is the fail-closed answer and must never be read as
absence — `host_git.rs:114`'s error shape is load-bearing, and
`host_git.rs:44`'s `cleanup_failed_add` remains V3-forbidden.

**4. Recovery-inventory coupling — this is why T3 was blocked on T2.** A
candidate whose preparation was transferred to recovery is still owned by a live
recovery flight (`preparation_recovery_flights`, populated by T2). Such a
candidate is **not** provably unused, however absent its target looks: the
recovery-owned runner may still be mid-operation. Consult the recovery inventory
in the proof and classify a recovery-owned candidate as **cannot-prove →
refuse**. State in your handoff exactly where you consult it and why that point
is race-free.

**5. Boot-caller wiring** for the decision path, still effect-free.

## Environment — read before you spend an attempt on it

Your container's egress allowlist permits **model APIs only**; crates.io is
deliberately absent (ADR-0013). `cargo` will fail with HTTP 403 on any uncached
dependency, so you have **no local compile loop**. This is by design, not a
fault to fix: do not edit proxy, egress, or Cargo configuration, and never
weaken or delete a test because you could not run it. The bridge's verify stage
compiles and runs the suite in a dependency-capable container; that is your
feedback channel.

**Falsification license.** Every claim in this spec — the anchors, the shape
mismatch, the recovery coupling — is an operator claim, not ground truth. If the
code does not match what is written here, say so plainly in your handoff with
the evidence, and do not force the change to fit the description.
