# R2f1b 3d T2 handoff — finite preparation ownership

## Scope

T2 adds the first producer of `PreparationFlightStateV1::Transferred` for a materialization flight whose injected preparation bound expires before the prepared barrier.

## Design

- A flight is unarmed in production; only the test seam provides `PreparationClockV1`.
- The bound records the 30-second action and 31-second observable-control values in the redacted durable transfer reason.
- A transfer publishes `Transferred`, returns the observing configure attempt with a typed `ConfigInvalid` pre-effect refusal, and moves the exact active owner into `preparation_recovery_flights`.
- The recovery handle owns the runner `JoinHandle`, so an in-flight/nonreturning blocking operation is neither detached nor dropped. T3 is the first production consumer.

## Blocking-operation population

| Operation | Boundary treatment |
|---|---|
| journal open / publish / sync | Samples after durable Open; expiry transfers before custody or provider admission. |
| custody entry / prepared publication / sync | Samples before entry and permits an observing transfer while the blocking sync is still owned. |
| identity capture / sync | Named for the full population, but occurs after `BarrierSynced`; expiry is deliberately non-transferring. |

## Tests and red-first evidence

- `nonreturning_custody_sync_transfers_pre_effect_owner`: removing the transfer call leaves `Open`, no recovery owner, and a configure that cannot terminalize while custody stalls.
- `preparation_bound_at_journal_open_publish_sync_transfers_before_any_effect`: omitting its boundary transfer yields `Settled` and one provider add instead of `Transferred` and zero effects.
- `unadvanced_preparation_clock_settles_normally`: changing the bound comparison to zero breaks the expected `Settled` control.
- `advanced_clock_after_prepared_barrier_does_not_transfer`: removing the barrier guard transfers after durable custody preparation.
- `production_default_does_not_arm_a_preparation_bound`: arming a bound in `WorktreeBackend::new` makes the deliberately slow control transfer.

## Frozen decisions

- No production timers, watcher tasks, or `tokio::time` are added to the materialization path.
- T1's caller-departure sample, cleanup join, and terminal-publication completion-debt handling remain intact.

## Closure repair — transfer/barrier linearization

### Phase and ownership design

`MaterializationPreparationFlightV1` now has one sticky CAS phase:
`Preparing -> TransferPublishing | BarrierPublishing`. Every pre-effect transfer takes the
first arm; the runner takes the second only after the immediate post-return sample for custody
preparation. The losing path cannot publish another preparation terminal or admit
`add_under_custody`. A failed `Transferred` write remains `TransferPublishing` active debt,
rather than reopening barrier admission.

Transfer first writes the durable terminal through the control journal, registers the exact active
owner in recovery without replacement, removes it from active, and only then wakes the configure
caller. The normal runner checks that transfer ownership before final completion, so releasing a
runner that lost the race cannot overwrite `Transferred` with `Settled` or `Failed`.

The pre-existing T1 caller-departure and cleanup-join behavior is unchanged: its independent
caller-admission CAS still commits a runner exactly once. The new phase CAS is orthogonal: it
chooses the sole pre-effect terminal/admission owner. Terminal-publication debt still remains in
the active owner until cleanup or retirement observes it; failed transfer publication deliberately
does not manufacture recovery success.

### Control journal

The `PreparationFlightJournalV1` control root is opened and installed on the flight before the
runner enters `JournalOpenPublish`. It remains the worktree control directory, not the target file
being opened or materialized. If the first `Open` write is blocked, transfer publishes
`Transferred` as the first control record; if Open won the filesystem race, transfer replaces only
that exact Open record. The released initial runner observes the sticky transfer phase and exits,
with its original join handle retained by recovery.

### Regressions and red-first evidence

- `preparation_phase_linearizes_transfer_and_barrier_races` drives transfer-first and
  barrier-first orders. Mutating either CAS back into independent booleans permits the opposite
  writer and fails the single-terminal/zero-add assertion.
- `slow_returning_custody_operation_transfers_before_barrier_admission` advances the manual clock
  from 29.9 s to 30.1 s while custody is blocked, releases it without the test observer, and
  requires `Transferred`, `ConfigInvalid`, and zero adds. Removing the post-return sample instead
  settles and admits one add.
- `blocked_initial_open_transfers_through_control_journal` blocks inside initial Open publication;
  removing the pre-runner control journal leaves no durable transfer and no recovery-owned exact
  runner.
- `transfer_registers_recovery_before_publishing_configure_result` pauses after the result send;
  moving completion ahead of recovery makes its inventory assertion fail.
- `panicking_provider_terminalizes_the_preparation_runner` removes the retained-sender hang by
  requiring a typed `AgentCrashed` and durable `Failed` record after provider panic.
- `transfer_terminal_publication_failure_retains_active_debt_without_recovery` injects failure of
  `Transferred`, requiring `StoreFailure`, retained active debt, no recovery entry, and zero
  provider effects.
